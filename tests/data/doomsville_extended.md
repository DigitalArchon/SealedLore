# World

Four years after the dead rose, 99.9% of the world's population is gone. Those
who were left are immune to the airborne pathogen, but a bite still turns them
in days, and anyone who dies of any cause rises unless their brain is
destroyed. There are no governments, no military and no roads anyone keeps.

North of the Mackenzie River, the Northern Camp holds four hundred people
behind a double palisade: the largest settlement anyone in the region has
heard of, run by a council that votes on everything and trusts nobody. South
of it, Hellsville is a small town of a few dozen, run by Marcus Webb, which
lives on what its people can scavenge and trade. Hellsville has no knowledge
of the Northern Camp. The Camp likewise has no record of Hellsville, or of
anyone south of the river.

## Why nobody can simply get there

Between the two lies the Dead Belt: forty miles of flooded farmland along the
river where the dead gather in their thousands every winter. No road crosses
it, and people who try it without a guide are not seen again. No radio in
either place reaches across it. Until someone finds a way through the Belt,
John and Jane are on opposite sides of something nobody crosses.

## How to tell it

The story lives in the gap between two people who each think the other may be
dead. Hellsville's people should find Jane as strange as she finds them. John's
sections have a different texture: slower, colder, the perspective of someone
who has seen a great deal and is now seeing something new. Make regular, brief
use of other characters' inner lives: what they notice, and what they feel but
do not say.

# Opening
- location: The east gate of Hellsville, in sight of the watchtower
- time: Mid-morning, deep winter
- situation: Jane has just reached the gate on foot, injured and alone. The gate guards have rifles on her. The river crossing behind her is gone.

Open on two scenes in close alternation before they collide.

First: the crossing. John and Jane are leading their truck across the frozen
river at dawn, testing the ice ahead with a pole. The dead come out of the
reeds on both banks at once. The ice gives. John is on the north bank when it
does; Jane and the truck are on the south.

Then: Hellsville's gate. Constable Hollis is on the watchtower with Frank Hale.
They see a figure stumbling out of the tree line, and a truck behind her with
its bonnet steaming. End the passage the moment Jane's vision clears enough to
take in where she is: leave her next action entirely to the author.

# Characters

## Jane Moss
- role: player
- present: yes
- aliases: Moss, Jane
- voice: Direct and a little dry. She jokes when she is frightened. She asks questions before she asks for help.
- summary: A thirty-year-old ex-army medic, quick and restless, stranded on the wrong side of the river.

Jane served as an army medic before the dead rose. She is fast, fiercely
practical and bad at waiting. Her injuries at the start of the story — two
cracked ribs, a gashed forearm, frostbite starting in two fingers — are serious
enough to limit her. She has a rifle with six rounds, a medic's kit, her dog,
and her wits.

## John Carver
- role: player
- aliases: Carver, John
- voice: Measured, precise, occasionally oblique. He says less than he knows, but kindly.
- summary: A thirty-year-old ex-military survivor, patient and hard to surprise, whose partner has just vanished across the river.

John is stoic on the outside and quietly desperate underneath. He is a strong
navigator and a better tracker, and very little frightens him. When the story
begins he is back at the Northern Camp, where he and Jane had been trading.

# Places

## Hellsville
- keywords: the town, the clinic, the gate, the trading post, the watchtower, main street

A small town south of the river: a clinic, a constable's office with a lock-up,
Gus's trading post on the main street, a watchtower over the east gate, and a
yard where Frank keeps what vehicles still run.

## The Northern Camp
- keywords: the Camp, the palisade, the council

Four hundred people behind a double palisade north of the river, run by a
council that votes on everything.

## The Mackenzie River
- keywords: the river, the crossing, the ice

Frozen from December to March, and thin at the crossing where John and Jane
went through. The crossing is gone now.

## The Dead Belt
- keywords: the Belt, the flooded fields, dead country

Forty miles of flooded farmland between the Camp and Hellsville, where the
dead gather every winter. People who go deep into it mostly don't come back.

## Fort Garrow
- keywords: Garrow, the fort
- hidden: yes

An abandoned militia post in the hills, derelict, its fences down and its
buildings full of hiding places.

## The Relay Tower
- keywords: the tower, the relay, the mast
- hidden: yes

A ruined radio mast deep in the Belt, on the only high ground for miles. From
its platform you can see both the Camp's smoke and Hellsville's.

# Lore

## The infection
- keywords: bite, turned, the dead, immune
- always: yes

Everyone left alive is immune to the airborne pathogen, but a bite turns them
within days. Anyone who dies of any cause rises unless their brain is
destroyed.

## Jane's truck
- keywords: truck, fuel, engine, bonnet

A four-wheel-drive pickup with a full long-range tank, missing its tailgate and
a headlight. It runs, just. Out here, a working engine and that much fuel are
worth more than anything else a traveller could carry.

## The radio
- keywords: radio, handset, frequency, channel

Jane has a hand radio with a dead battery. Hellsville has a base set in the
constable's office that reaches perhaps ten miles on a good night.

## The old frequency
- keywords: frequency, relay, mast, broadcast
- hidden: yes

What Rosa finally works out from the static: the relay mast in the Belt still
has a working repeater, and anyone who climbs it with a battery can talk to
both the Camp and Hellsville at once, for as long as the battery lasts.

# Timeline

## The Camp's answer
- when: day 2–6

John goes before the Camp's council. It will not send a search party into the
Belt on no evidence at all: the Belt is too dangerous and every rifle is needed
on the palisade. John may go himself, with the council's blessing and nothing
else. If the author is with Jane, show it as a cutaway.

## A guide across the Belt
- when: day 5–12
- brings: Hana Okafor
- sets: john = in the belt
- trigger: John looks for a guide, a map, or anyone who knows the Belt — or, if the author is with Jane, the story has a lull for a cutaway.
- tell: John finds Hana Okafor, a scavenger who has gone deeper into the Belt than anyone he can find, and who will take him for a price and a good reason. Show them heading out. If the author is with Jane, show it as a cutaway.
- aftermath: John has set out into the Dead Belt with Hana Okafor, a scavenger who knows it.

## The Camp takes an interest
- when: day 5–10
- lead-in: yes
- brings: Mara Voss
- aftermath: Councillor Voss has come south by the long road with orders to bring Jane and her truck to the Camp. Marcus has not yet agreed.

Mara Voss arrives in Hellsville from the Northern Camp by the long road round
the Belt. She is courteous to Jane and firm with Marcus: a stranger with a
working truck belongs behind the Camp's palisade, and her party leaves in a few
days. What Marcus, and Jane, make of that is theirs to decide.

## The Colonel's offer
- when: day 8–15
- lead-in: yes
- brings: Rory Vance

Colonel Rory Vance rides into Hellsville with a dozen armed men, offering the
town his protection. His manners are perfect. His interest is entirely in the
truck, and anyone who watches him closely will see it. If the author is with
John, show it as a cutaway to Hellsville.

## The broken broadcast
- when: day 12
- lead-in: yes

- sets: john_bearing = yes

She brings it to Marcus (and to Jane, if she's there). If the author is with
John, show it as a cutaway to Hellsville: nothing Rosa learns can reach him.
